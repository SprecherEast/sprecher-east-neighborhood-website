## `KeyFacts`

A row of small fact cards (#509, #660), after the USWDS icon list and summary box: an icon beside what the fact is, a big short value, how sure we are in words, and the source (`FactCard`). In the shared `CardGrid`, a list, so screen readers hear how many facts there are. The cards share their rows (subgrid), so the values and sources line up even when one title wraps (#678). The row gap is zero because a shared row's gap would sit between a card's own rows: each card keeps its distance from the next one below with a bottom margin, which the list's negative margin takes back at the end.

## `FactCard`

One fact as a small card (#509, #660, #674): the icon beside what the fact is about, in bold body-size text, on the first row; the big short value under it; an optional one-line explanation; then how sure we are in words and the source. The same order on screen and for screen readers (`dt`, then `dd`s). The icon sits inside the `dt`, decorative, and aligned to the label's first line so a label that wraps doesn't centre it awkwardly. Drawn here so the key facts row and any later block that shows a fact look the same. `variant="tab"` (#664) is the same fact as the face of a tab button: the same rows, but in phrasing elements with no card, since the tab button is the box (its border, selected state and focus ring), and with no link, because a link can't sit inside a button. The source link is drawn by whoever shows the tab's panel. A tab has the same parts for every fact (#707): the explanation is left to the open panel, so one fact having one doesn't make its tab fuller than the rest. The icon sits on its own line with the label under it (#856), centred on a phone and left-aligned from `sm` up, so a short label fits four to a row. On a phone (#704) the certainty is left to screen readers (`sr-only`) and the open panel shows it in a line. The figure keeps its last two words together, so "+$90 a year" wraps as "+$90" over "a year".

## `Timeline`

Milestones on a vertical line, oldest first (#507), following the MOJ and DWP timeline: the date leads each entry beside its marker. A milestone's status shows three ways: the marker's shape (filled, ringed, dashed, hollow), the line to it (solid once reached, dashed ahead) and a labelled badge, so it reads without colour. The markers and line are decoration; the list, dates, titles and badges carry the meaning for screen readers.

## `OutcomeFork`

One question branching to two or three outcomes (#510). On wide screens the outcomes sit side by side under the question, joined by a stem and a bar; on phones they stack, hung from a line down the left. The connectors are decoration: the question is a heading and the outcomes a list, each title read with its tone in words ("Goes ahead: If they agree"), since the icon is hidden.
