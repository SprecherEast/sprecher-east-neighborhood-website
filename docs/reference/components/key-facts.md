## `KeyFacts`

A row of small fact cards (#509, #660), after the USWDS icon list and summary box: an icon beside what the fact is, a big short value, how sure we are in words, and the source (`FactCard`). In the shared `CardGrid`, a list, so screen readers hear how many facts there are. The cards share their rows (subgrid), so the values and sources line up even when one title wraps (#678). The row gap is zero because a shared row's gap would sit between a card's own rows: each card keeps its distance from the next one below with a bottom margin, which the list's negative margin takes back at the end.

## `FactCard`

One fact as a small card (#509, #660, #674): the icon beside what the fact is about, in bold body-size text, on the first row; the big short value under it; an optional one-line explanation; then how sure we are in words and the source. The same order on screen and for screen readers (`dt`, then `dd`s). The icon sits inside the `dt`, decorative, and aligned to the label's first line so a label that wraps doesn't centre it awkwardly. Drawn here so the key facts row and any later block that shows a fact look the same. `variant="tab"` (#664, #1112) is the face of a tab button: only the label shows, in phrasing elements, since the tab button is the box (its border, selected state and focus ring), and with no link, because a link can't sit inside a button. The tab's figure and how sure we are are visually hidden text after the label, so screen readers still hear them from the button; the open panel shows both (`FactTabs`). With a `tabId`, `{yearly}` and `{monthly}` in the figure follow the tab's whole tax bill (#1029).

## `FactTabs`

## `BillShare`

The whole property-tax bill at the top of a fact tab (#1018, #1111), one column on every width, in the order a neighbor needs it: the bill block's one intro line, the Rough Estimator (the home value the reader can change, which starts at the City's average home, and the switch between tax years), the answer card (the City's tax on the home and year, its increase, the year's status), the whole bill (its own card: the total, one bar whose slices are as wide as their shares, and the receipt naming each), then one card of three rows: where the City's share goes (the split alone), why the bill may differ and the sources. The tab's other pictures (`children`) follow. Everything is drawn again for every home value and year, so every amount is the City's slice of the bill. A year in which a body has not set its rate uses that body's rate from the year before, and says so in a note whose room is always kept, so the page never changes height.

## `BillAnswer`

The answer a neighbor came for (#1111, #1118), right under the Rough Estimator. Its top row has the label on the left ("City tax on an average home", or "on your home" after an estimate) and the status badge on the right. Under it the City's tax on the home last estimated in the year chosen is the biggest text on the tab, with a small "a year", then the City's increase in the estimator's accent, orange (`increaseLine`, the same figures a tab's `{yearly}` and `{monthly}` use, so they always agree) and one small sentence with the home value used and the City's share of the whole bill. In an earlier year the sentence says the value is the one assumed for that year (#1121). The badge is the chosen year's stored note, with the "not final" look, or, when the year has none, the tab's stored certainty, so it can never disagree with the year. The increase line keeps its room when there is no figure, so the card never changes height. In the earliest of several years it reads "Base year" in the primary green instead, with the next year's line kept as hidden room (#1135). It draws what it is given.

## `BillDetails`

The three rows under the whole bill (#1111, #1118, #1121), in one card split by divider lines: where the City's share goes (`where`: the breakdown chart, drawn for the home and year shown), why the real bill may differ (the notes, then the tab's short text and detail) and the sources, each source once. The first row is open when the tab loads, with scripting off too; the other two are closed. A row with nothing to show is left out, and a note that is not showing still takes its room, hidden from everyone, so an open row never changes height when a year is chosen. Each row is the kit's `Disclosure`, so it opens and closes without scripting and never moves what is above it.

## `RoughEstimator`

The tool at the top of a whole-bill picture (#1022): a compact orange card, named Rough Estimator for screen readers but with no title drawn, with the home value box and, on the same row at every width, the tax years as one joined two-option control of the year numbers, as tall as the box (#1118). The box has a check-mark button inside it at its end (#1110): off, flat and muted, while the typed value is not a new number, and on, accent-colored and slightly raised once it is (Enter in the box does the same), so on a phone it sits right above the keyboard (#1087). Nothing is drawn under the years: the answer card's badge says a year's status. It draws what it is given and keeps no state of its own.

## `BillFiguresProvider`
