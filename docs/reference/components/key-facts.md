## `KeyFacts`

A row of small fact cards (#509, #660), after the USWDS icon list and summary box: an icon beside what the fact is, a big short value, how sure we are in words, and the source (`FactCard`). In the shared `CardGrid`, a list, so screen readers hear how many facts there are. The cards share their rows (subgrid), so the values and sources line up even when one title wraps (#678). The row gap is zero because a shared row's gap would sit between a card's own rows: each card keeps its distance from the next one below with a bottom margin, which the list's negative margin takes back at the end.

## `FactCard`

One fact as a small card (#509, #660, #674): the icon beside what the fact is about, in bold body-size text, on the first row; the big short value under it; an optional one-line explanation; then how sure we are in words and the source. The same order on screen and for screen readers (`dt`, then `dd`s). The icon sits inside the `dt`, decorative, and aligned to the label's first line so a label that wraps doesn't centre it awkwardly. Drawn here so the key facts row and any later block that shows a fact look the same. `variant="tab"` (#664, #1112) is the face of a tab button: only the label shows, in phrasing elements, since the tab button is the box (its border, selected state and focus ring), and with no link, because a link can't sit inside a button. The tab's figure and how sure we are are visually hidden text after the label, so screen readers still hear them from the button; the open panel shows both (`FactTabs`). With a `tabId`, `{yearly}` and `{monthly}` in the figure follow the tab's whole tax bill (#1029).

## `SectionTabs`

An article split into tabs of ordinary content (#1333): the shared `Tabs` (equal label-only buttons in one row, the content directly under them in the same column, every panel on the server so with scripting off they read one after another under their labels). The content of a tab arrives drawn, so this knows nothing of rich text. It clears any floated picture before it, so the buttons and what they open stay in one column under an introduction and its picture. It is not `not-prose`: the content of a tab is article text, and Tailwind's typography skips everything under a `not-prose` ancestor, even a nested `prose`.

## `BillAnswer`

The answer a neighbor came for (#1111, #1118), right under the Rough Estimator. Its top row has the label on the left ("City tax on an average home", or "on your home" after an estimate) and the status badge on the right. Under it the City's tax on the home last estimated in the year chosen is the biggest text on the tab, with a small "a year", then the City's increase in the estimator's accent, orange (`increaseLine`, the same figures a tab's `{yearly}` and `{monthly}` use, so they always agree) and, while the card shows the City's average home, one small sentence with that home's value and the City's share of the whole bill. After an estimate the sentence is left out: it would only repeat the value the reader typed and a share the whole bill shows (#1183). The badge is the chosen year's stored note, with the "not final" look, or, when the year has none, the tab's stored certainty, so it can never disagree with the year. The increase line keeps its room when there is no figure, so the card never changes height. In the earliest of several years it reads "Base year" in the primary green instead, with the next year's line kept as hidden room (#1135). It draws what it is given.

## `RatesPanel`

The rates the bill is worked out from (#1351), beside the answer card: one line per body with its name and the year its rate is from on the left, and its rate as a percent with the same rate in dollars per $1,000 of value on the right. The City's line is highlighted, since the tab is about the City's tax. Every line is built the same way and as tall as the others, so the lines sit level in every year and the panel never changes height; a body with no rate keeps its line, with "No rate yet" in place of the figures. The year each rate is from is on every line, so the panel never depends on the toast (`toast`, #1380) that says why: the page puts it over the panel's top edge (the card is its positioned container), where it covers a sliver and takes no room, so the panel is as tall in every year. It draws what it is given.

## `BillDetails`

The two rows under the whole bill (#1111, #1118, #1163), in one card split by divider lines: why the real bill may differ (the notes, then the tab's short text and detail) and the sources, each source once, both closed when the tab loads, each titled from the block (#1158). Where the City's share goes is a row of the whole bill itself, under the City's row (`BillShare`, #1163). Each row is the kit's `Disclosure`, so it opens and closes without scripting and never moves what is above it.

## `RoughEstimator`

The tool at the top of a whole-bill picture (#1022): a compact orange card, named Rough Estimator for screen readers but with no title drawn, with the home value box and, on the same row at every width, the tax years as one joined two-option control of the year numbers, as tall as the box (#1118). The box has a check-mark button inside it at its end (#1110): off, flat and muted, while the typed value is not a new number, and on, accent-colored and slightly raised once it is (Enter in the box does the same), so on a phone it sits right above the keyboard (#1087). Nothing is drawn under the years: the answer card's badge says a year's status. It draws what it is given and keeps no state of its own.
